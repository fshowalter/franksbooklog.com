<p>Not every book gets a rating. I used to finish every book I started, but not anymore. I’m embracing the abandon.</p>
<hr>
<h2>On Abandoning Books</h2>
<p>Fifty-three pages into the 320-page novel, I closed it and moved on.</p>
<p>It wasn’t easy.</p>
<p>Finishing books brought a sense of accomplishment—a comfort in completing a task, checking a box, achieving a goal. Letting go of that illusory security is hard.</p>
<p>But I’m learning.</p>
<p>Forcing myself to finish every book didn’t make me a better person. It didn’t make me smarter or more well-rounded. It just made reading a chore rather than a pleasure.</p>
<p>I’m old enough that I’ve got more life behind me than ahead, which means I’ll never finish all the books I want to read.</p>
<p>It’s about opportunity cost. I loved the pulp storytelling of Graham Masterton’s <span data-title-id="the-devils-of-d-day-by-graham-masterton"><em>The Devils of D-Day</em></span>. Mark Richard’s writing in <span data-title-id="the-ice-at-the-bottom-of-the-world-by-mark-richard"><em>The Ice at the Bottom of the World</em></span> floored me. Clive Barker’s imagination made <em>The Books of Blood</em> unforgettable. Knowing there are more books out there like those, while I’m forcing myself to trudge through a mediocre read, pushed me to abandon lesser efforts.</p>
<p>My shelves hold hundreds of unread books. I’d wager that a quarter of them aren’t worth reading. Maybe more.</p>
<p>Some folks give a book a chapter or two. Others give a book a set number of pages. Neither system makes sense to me. I’ve had books start strong then flounder. And how does one apply an arbitrary page count to books of varying lengths? Fifty pages is a quarter of a two hundred page novel, but only five percent of a thousand-page biography.</p>
<p>I give a book three hours. The length of a long movie. If a book drags for longer, I’ll abandon it. This doesn’t mean I have to <em>finish</em> it in three hours, just that it can’t go longer than three consecutive hours without holding my interest. Granted, this means I could abandon a thousand-page doorstop with under a hundred pages to go, but I’m okay with that. Failing to recognize a sunk cost is a gambler’s fallacy.</p>
<p>Perhaps, as I discover better books, my standards will increase. Today’s excellence might be tomorrow’s schlock. Of course, that would see me abandoning even more books.</p>
<p>I can only hope.</p>
